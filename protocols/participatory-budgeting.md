# Participatory Budgeting

## Summary

A democratic process where community members directly decide how to spend part of a public budget. Participants propose, discuss, and vote on spending priorities, giving ordinary people real power over real money.

## Purpose

Participatory budgeting (PB) democratizes public finance by:
- Giving citizens direct decision-making power
- Increasing transparency in budget allocation
- Prioritizing community-identified needs
- Building civic capacity and engagement

## Origin

Porto Alegre, Brazil, 1989. The Workers' Party government implemented PB to address inequality and corruption. The process spread globally, now used in thousands of cities on every continent.

## Core Process

### Annual Cycle

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                 │
│   IDEA COLLECTION ──────► PROPOSAL DEVELOPMENT  │
│        (2-3 months)           (1-2 months)      │
│                                                 │
│              ▲                      │           │
│              │                      ▼           │
│                                                 │
│   IMPLEMENTATION ◄────── VOTING                 │
│        (12 months)           (1 month)          │
│                                                 │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Phase 1: Idea Collection (2-3 months)

**Community Assemblies**
- Open meetings in neighborhoods
- Residents propose project ideas
- No idea rejected at this stage

**Online Submission**
- Web platform for ideas
- Accessible 24/7
- Optional anonymity

**Outreach**
- Active outreach to underrepresented groups
- Multilingual materials
- Meetings at accessible times/locations

### Phase 2: Proposal Development (1-2 months)

**Budget Delegates**
- Volunteer community members
- Meet with city staff
- Develop feasible proposals from ideas

**Technical Review**
- City departments assess feasibility
- Cost estimates developed
- Legal/regulatory review

**Proposal Refinement**
- Delegates shape final proposals
- Multiple proposals may merge
- Community input continues

### Phase 3: Voting (1 month)

**Voting Methods**
- In-person at community sites
- Online/mobile voting
- Paper ballots with collection

**Eligibility**
- Typically: residents of the area
- Often includes: youth (14+), immigrants, etc.
- Varies by jurisdiction

**Vote Counting**
- Projects ranked by votes
- Funded in order until budget exhausted
- Results published publicly

### Phase 4: Implementation (12 months)

**Project Execution**
- City departments implement
- Community oversight committees
- Regular progress updates

**Monitoring**
- Budget delegates track progress
- Public dashboard of project status
- Year-end evaluation

## Budget Allocation

### Typical Budget Sources
- Capital improvement funds
- Community development block grants
- Discretionary municipal funds
- Special district revenues

### Budget Scale Examples
| City | Annual PB Budget | Population |
|------|------------------|------------|
| Paris | €100 million | 2.1 million |
| New York | $40 million | 8.3 million |
| Cambridge, MA | $1.5 million | 118,000 |
| Porto Alegre | ~15% of investment budget | 1.5 million |

## Variations

### Digital PB
Heavy use of online tools for all phases. Increases reach but may exclude digitally marginalized.

### Youth PB
Students decide school or youth program budgets. Builds civic capacity early.

### Housing PB
Tenants in public housing decide improvement budgets.

### Workplace PB
Employees decide spending on workplace improvements.

## Success Factors

**Political Will**
- Real budget, real power
- Leadership commitment
- Multi-year commitment

**Inclusive Outreach**
- Proactive, not passive
- Resources for participation (childcare, translation, transportation)
- Meeting marginalized communities where they are

**Clear Rules**
- Transparent process
- Published criteria
- Consistent application

**Technical Support**
- Staff dedicated to PB
- Feasibility analysis capacity
- Implementation capability

## Challenges

**Elite Capture**
- More organized groups dominate
- Mitigation: quotas, targeted outreach

**Sustainability**
- Political transitions threaten continuity
- Mitigation: institutionalize in law/charter

**Scale**
- Large populations strain participation
- Mitigation: district-level processes

**Deliberation vs. Voting**
- Quick voting without discussion
- Mitigation: deliberative forums before voting

## Impact Evidence

**Porto Alegre (1989-2004)**
- Sewerage coverage: 49% → 85%
- Schools: 29 → 86
- Health centers: 13 → 114
- Paved roads doubled

**New York City (2011-present)**
- $250+ million allocated
- 100,000+ voters annually
- 700+ projects funded

**Research Findings**
- Increased spending on low-income neighborhoods
- Higher civic engagement beyond PB
- More trust in government
