# Sociocracy

## Summary

A governance protocol organizing groups into semi-autonomous circles connected by double-links, using consent-based decision making for policy. Also known as "dynamic governance," sociocracy enables effective self-organization at scale while maintaining equivalence among members.

## Purpose

Sociocracy addresses the challenge of governing organizations democratically while remaining effective. It balances the need for distributed authority with coordination across the whole.

## Core Principles

### 1. Consent Governs Policy

All policy decisions are made by consent - no reasoned objections. This replaces both autocratic and majority-rule approaches.

### 2. Circles

The organization is structured in semi-autonomous circles. Each circle:
- Has a clear domain (area of responsibility)
- Makes its own policy decisions within that domain
- Chooses its own members for roles
- Is connected to other circles through linking

### 3. Double-Linking

Circles are connected through two links:
- The **Leader** is selected by the parent circle to represent its needs in the child circle
- The **Delegate** is selected by the child circle to represent it in the parent circle

Both participate fully in both circles, ensuring information flows both ways.

### 4. Elections by Consent

Rather than voting, roles are filled through consent-based election:
1. Members write nominations with reasons
2. Nominations are read aloud
3. Members can change nominations based on what they heard
4. Discussion of candidates
5. Consent round on final proposal

## Implementation Steps

### Phase 1: Preparation (1-2 months)
1. Leadership team learns sociocracy deeply
2. Assess organizational readiness
3. Create implementation plan
4. Communicate to all members

### Phase 2: Foundation (2-3 months)
1. Establish top circle
2. Define mission, vision, aims
3. Create initial circle structure
4. Select initial leaders and delegates

### Phase 3: Practice (3-6 months)
1. Circles begin meeting with consent process
2. Regular coaching and support
3. Adjust structure as needed
4. Celebrate successes and learn from challenges

### Phase 4: Integration (Ongoing)
1. Sociocracy becomes "how we work"
2. Continuous improvement of process
3. Spread learning to new members
4. Connect with other sociocratic organizations

## Meeting Formats

### Circle Meeting (Regular)

1. **Opening Round**: Check-in, presence
2. **Administrative**: Approve minutes, review actions
3. **Content**: Policy proposals, reports, discussions
4. **Closing Round**: Meeting evaluation

### Consent Decision Process

1. **Present Proposal**: Clear, written proposal
2. **Clarifying Questions**: Understanding only
3. **Quick Reactions**: Initial responses
4. **Amend & Clarify**: Proposer updates
5. **Consent Round**: Go around for consent/objection
6. **Celebrate**: Acknowledge the decision

## Roles in Circles

| Role | Selected By | Function |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Leader | Parent circle | Operational lead, external link to parent |
| Delegate | Own circle | Represents circle in parent |
| Facilitator | Own circle | Facilitates meetings |
| Secretary | Own circle | Manages records and logistics |

## Variations

### Sociocracy 3.0 (S3)
A modular approach taking patterns from sociocracy without requiring full adoption.

### Holacracy
A more structured variant with detailed constitution and emphasis on "role" over "person."

## Success Factors

- **Commitment to learning**: Mistakes are expected, learning is required
- **Clear domains**: Each circle needs defined scope
- **Regular meetings**: Circles typically meet every 1-2 weeks
- **Training**: Initial and ongoing education
- **Patience**: Full implementation takes 1-3 years

## Challenges

- **Cultural shift**: Moving from hierarchy or consensus
- **Meeting discipline**: Consent process requires practice
- **Scaling**: Large organizations need careful circle design
- **Leadership**: Different skills needed than traditional management

## Case Studies

### Housing Cooperative (50 members)
Transitioned to sociocracy over 18 months. Circles for maintenance, finance, membership, community. Reported 60% reduction in meeting time while increasing participation satisfaction.

### Nonprofit Organization (15 staff)
Adopted sociocracy to address founder dependency. Created program, operations, and governance circles. Successfully transitioned leadership after 2 years.
